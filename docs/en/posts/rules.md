# Ride rules

These rules cover basic safety on our rides. Ignore them and sooner or later someone ends up in the ER, so the ride leader can ask you to leave the group if you break them.

We wrote them to help you get into the cycling scene safely and have a great time doing it, and so you don't accidentally take someone out just because nobody told you.

How we look and how we talk to each other is covered in a separate post: [Vibe and etiquette](/en/posts/etiquette). How to ride in a group - see the [group riding guide](/en/posts/group-riding).

## Gear

No start without these.

### 1. Helmet - must have

Only riders wearing a working, buckled-up bike helmet can join the ride. The helmet comes off only when we're stopped.

Full-face, motorcycle, BMX and other unsuitable helmets are a no too: you can't see well in them, it's awkward to eat and drink on the move, your head cooks inside, and you'll want to take it off on the first climb.

### 2. Handlebars - drop bars only

Any bike with drop bars will do: road, gravel, cyclocross, drop-bar MTB.

Exceptions:

- **MTB** is fine only if the ride announcement says so explicitly.
- **Flat bars** are allowed in very rare cases, by agreement with the ride leader, and only for experienced, strong riders we know. That rider rides at the very back, keeps a gap to the group and never, under any circumstances, rides next to another cyclist.

Not allowed on any ride, ever: TT and triathlon bikes, any aero bars or clip-on extensions, and extra bar ends.

### 3. Two brakes and a freewheel

Your bike must have two working brakes and a freewheel. The exception is fixie rides, if the announcement says so explicitly.

### 4. Bar plugs

The ends of your bars must be closed with bar plugs. In a crash, an open bar end works like a hole punch, and nobody wants to test that on themselves or the rider next to them.

### 5. Nothing sticking out

Selfie sticks, telescopic camera mounts, pegs and any other gadgets sticking out past the bike stay at home.

### 6. Lights after dark

White light in front, red at the back. Charged, obviously.

### 7. Bike in good shape

Tires pumped, brakes that actually brake, nothing rattling or creaking. If in doubt, show your bike to the ride leader before the start and we'll sort it out together.

## Behavior

### 8. Traffic lights and intersections

We're not a motorcade with flashing lights, and the road isn't just ours. Everyone is responsible for how they get through an intersection.

**Pedestrians and cross traffic are sacred.** Never ride through people crossing or cut through a line of cars, no matter how much you don't want to get dropped. At crosswalks without lights, pedestrians always go first.

**Red lights.** We try not to run reds. If the intersection or crossing is quiet, you can see everything clearly and there are definitely no people or cars around, you can carefully go through as if it were a Yield sign: slow down, look, go. But that's your call, and so are the fines and any other consequences. "The guys in front went through" is not an argument - we don't follow the group through a red blindly.

**No squeezing through.** If there are up to five cars in a lane at a red, don't squeeze between the lanes; wait behind them like a regular car. Otherwise the drivers have to pass the same bunch of cyclists all over again, and that won't make them love us any more. If there are more cars than that, filter through slowly, one at a time and only between stopped cars, passing them on the left: on the right, in town, someone's always opening a door, turning, or pulling into a parking spot. Never squeeze alongside moving cars, under any circumstances.

**Group got split - wait up.** If the group gets split at a light, a turn or any other maneuver in traffic, the front part slows down or stops and waits until everyone's back together. That way nobody has to run a red to catch up.

### 9. Music - one ear, low volume

One earbud or bone-conduction headphones at low volume are fine. Two regular earbuds are not.

The reason is simple: in a group you need to hear the calls and the cars. Plus it's just annoying when you say something to someone and they have to pause their music and ask you to repeat it.

Speakers are usually banned on rides: everyone around you would have to listen to the music you like, and you can't hear the calls over it either. The exceptions are sauna rides, or if the ride leader says it's OK.

### 10. Hands on the bars

**Phone.** If you're an experienced rider and can handle your bike confidently with one hand, it's fine to reply to a message or snap a photo on the move. If you're not sure you can, then you can't yet - do it at a stop.

Want a shot of the whole group? Take it from the next lane over on an empty road, after checking there are no cars behind you and no potholes ahead.

**No riding no-handed in the group.** One bad gust of wind or one pothole, and instead of a slick move you get a pile-up. Taking off your vest, putting on a jacket, or digging through your pockets with both hands is for stops - then catch up with the group. Or drop to the very back, make sure there's nobody behind you, and change there.

### 11. Ride leader

Whoever posts the ride announcement in the chat is the ride leader by default and is responsible for the whole ride. They can hand the role over to someone else if needed. Route, pace, format and stops are usually spelled out in the announcement, so ask your questions in the chat beforehand, not on the move. If the ride leader makes a call on the road, it's not up for debate; anything contentious we'll talk through at a stop, at the finish, or later in the chat.

The ride leader doesn't have to ride at the front: they might take turns with everyone else or hang at the back keeping an eye on the group. We ride as one group with them - no riding off ahead and no dragging the group along at your own pace. The exception is climbs and descents, where the group splits up anyway: there everyone rides their own pace. We usually regroup at the top of long climbs and after long descents, but where and when we wait for each other is stated in the announcement or agreed with the ride leader.

### 12. Leaving early? Say so

If you're leaving the route, tell the ride leader or someone in the group, and they'll pass it on. Otherwise the group will be standing around wondering where you went.

### 13. Know how to ride in a group

Basic group riding skills are mandatory: holding your line, pointing out obstacles, not overlapping wheels, taking and handing off turns at the front properly. It's all covered in the [group riding guide](/en/posts/group-riding). If it's your first group ride, read it beforehand and tell the ride leader you're new.

## Self-sufficiency

### 14. What to bring

- a spare tube or two;
- a pump or a CO₂ cartridge;
- a multitool;
- enough water and food for the whole route;
- your phone, a card and some cash: village shops often don't take cards, and ferries are cash only.

The group will always help out, but fixing your bike with your own tools is a lot nicer than going around asking who has a tube in your size.

## Health and responsibility

### 15. Ride at your own risk

We're a community, not a race organizer. Everyone is responsible for their own safety, health and gear.

### 16. Emergency contact

Add an emergency contact (ICE) to your phone so it shows up on the lock screen. If you have any health issues we should know about in case something goes wrong, tell the ride leader before the start.

### 17. Photos and videos

By showing up for a ride, you agree to being photographed and filmed, and to the club using those photos and videos for any creative non-commercial purposes. More on this in [Vibe and etiquette](/en/posts/etiquette#photos).

## If the rules are broken

The ride leader makes the call.

1. **A heads-up** on the move or at a stop.
2. **A warning** if it happens again.
3. **A request to leave the ride** if it keeps happening or is dangerous right away (for example, someone shows up without a helmet).
